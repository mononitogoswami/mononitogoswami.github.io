I study how large language models get better, and what stops them.

A language model system improves through three levers: the weights it learns in training, the compute it spends at test time, and the harness it runs in. At Amazon I work on all three, the same way each time: find the mechanism that limits the lever, measure it directly, then build the method that removes it.

In training, SCOUT shows that on-policy distillation hides an off-policy teacher, and fixes it by training the teacher on the student's own prefixes. At test time, Hermes shows that extra context windows hurt a small model until it learns what to use them for. In the harness, MILO makes harness design self-improving: an orchestrator diagnoses where the search has stalled and changes its strategy.

The habit comes from foundation models and evaluation science. I built MOMENT (ICML 2024, 2.5M+ downloads), one of the first open time-series foundation models, contributed to Chronos-2, and developed benchmarks and label-free model selection methods (TimeSeriesGym, AQuA, ICLR 2023 Spotlight). I also helped lead the science behind the AWS DevOps Agent.

I completed my Ph.D. in Robotics at Carnegie Mellon University (advised by Artur Dubrawski) and received the Robotics Institute Distinguished Dissertation Award. Previously at Google Research and AWS AI Labs.
