How do language models get better, and what stops them?

A language model system improves through three levers: the harness it runs in, the compute it spends at test time, and the weights it learns in training. Each project below starts with a measurement that exposes what limits one lever, then builds a method aimed at that mechanism. The figures are interactive and are rebuilt from the numbers in each paper.

Harness · Recursive self-improvement

MILO

Automated harness discovery usually stalls because the search strategy is fixed. MILO co-evolves the harness and its own search: an orchestrator diagnoses stalled islands and intervenes by grafting lineages and reassigning mutators. It reaches state-of-the-art results on Terminal-Bench 2.1, PaperBench and DeepSWE.

Test-time compute · Contextual reasoning

Hermes

Spreading test-time compute over many context windows means deciding what each fresh context should do and what to carry back. Hermes leaves those choices to the model. Out of the box, a 4B model gets worse with extra contexts; after Hermes-Learn (SFT then RL), it beats a single context with the same total token budget on all four math benchmarks.

Paper Project page, with rollouts to step through

Training · On-policy distillation

SCOUT

In on-policy distillation the student writes the response and the teacher grades every token. On-policy for the student is off-policy for the teacher: the deeper into a student's response, the less the text looks like anything the teacher would write. SCOUT measures this degradation directly, then trains the teacher with RL to continue student-generated prefixes.
